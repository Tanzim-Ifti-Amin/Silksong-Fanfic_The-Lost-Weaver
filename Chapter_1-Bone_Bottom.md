**<ins>Chapter 1 - The Bone Bottom<ins>**

I feel horrible. Those veiled bugs, the kidnapping from Hallownest…

The little Ghost’s empty mask.

It’s all too much.

Clink.
Clank.
Clang.

My needle! It nearly impaled me in the mask, goodness.
May as well bind with it, then.

I look around. A fairly peaceful area, covered in moss and greenery. In stark contrast to the White Palace, at least.

‘How did I get myself here? Should I even try to figure out, or do I take the easy path out?’ I mutter. I ponder. I don’t have time to waste. Ok, Hornet. Do a Silk Soar. I push my needle to the ground, crouch down, and try to summon Silk around me.

Nothing happens.

---

It’s as if I’ve forgotten everything I’d spent years training at Hallownest with. I test it out. I can’t summon Silk to perform a Silk Spear.
Even my signature Thread Storm is gone. I try to taunt whoever is around, but I can’t produce any new Silk from my shell. The only thing I can do is bind.

‘Am I so weak I can’t handle a single small concussion anymore?’ I begin to cut through the vines around me as I walk around this lush area. At least I can still swing around my needle. It is Pure Hivesteel, sharpened to a deadly edge, sharper than even the Nail of my dear brother the Hollow Knight.

I look at my perfect needle.
It’s devastated. Ruined. Tarnished. My most treasured gift, from the Queen of the Hive, Vespa herself. Blunted.

I hate this so much.

---

I keep walking, and find small, mossy , slightly spiky creatures. Quite sharp spikes, after closer inspection. Closer inspection being, trying to pet one and having my palm punctured by a handful of thorns. I’m stupid, of course it would hurt, they’re spikes. Has Father’s stupid saw-blade obsession taught me nothing? I stab my needle into the thing in rage. It is visibly wounded, but my pure needle would have decimated it.

I stab again, and again. I feel the silk build up in me as I kill more of these mossy things. Good for me, bad for them.
‘Have my silk glands been so badly damaged I can only get silk through death and violence?’

As I leave the room, I feel dizzy. That has to be karma for what I just did back there.
I nearly black out, but straighten myself back up.

A gap to cross. Surely it can’t be too difficult. I jump.
And I miss.
And fall.
I catch myself on a ledge right next to a string of something. A string of red things. I pick it up. The string is frayed and worn, but the red things are shiny. I pick it up. Very shiny, and smooth.

---

I remember.
The shininess of the Radiance’s orange infection, how it shimmered, a false light that only sought to destroy Hallownest.

Enough of these thoughts. The Radiance has been dead since the Little Ghost’s sacrifice. I hope he will one day repay me in some way for me steering him in the right path. He definitely owes me one after that, else he would’ve replaced Hollow.
I pocket the strange beads, even though they make me shiver when I see them.

I get back up to that jump after a lot of clambering, and make it this time. Excellent. I enter a new room. This is different. Hollow. The ground, walls, and ceiling. Skeletal. This place is the bottom of the world, and it seems very bony. I christen the place ‘Bone Bottom’.

I see a door, and try and sprint to it. Except I can’t. Because my dense brain forgot how to run.
The door looks like my mother’s mask. Herrah’s mask.

The little ghost, looking at me, asking consent to execute her without a voice. Because he was the one with no voice to cry suffering. Not Hollow. Not after that scream that called the children of the Abyss. I told him that I would let him do the deed, as long as he saved our kingdom, but I would not watch him do it. And I left the room to let my mother die.

---

The door seems to be operated by a mechanism that only opens using a harp. I try to prise the door open with my needle. It groans, and I realise it’s going to snap if I keep trying. So I make a mental note, and leave for now.
The next room is simply a hallway, and returns to the mossy, overgrown aesthetic from before. Nothing interesting other than a tangle of vines at the end and what looks like a bony room.

Chopping down the thick vines with my already suffering needle, I find myself in a small structure. I spot a snail’s shell modelled out of rock, leaning against a wall. The symbol of a dwelling of a Snail Shaman.
The little ghost had an entire collection of charms shaped like that. At the Kingdom’s Edge, when we fought a fierce battle, I saw him wearing them all at the same time. When he defeated me, and became stuck in the Cast-away Shell, I knew he was worthy. And I rescued him.

I saw him fighting The Hollow Knight with those shaman charms on.

My knees buckle as I struggle to stay upright. Eventually, my weakened body feels like collapsing. I fight it, knowing that having to form a cocoon when I wasn’t even in combat would severely hurt my personal pride. I get up, and see a stone bench, like the one the Elderbug used to talk about, assuring an iron bench could also be comfortable. I sit on it, and it does seem quite nice. I focus and set myself to come here if I ever form a cocoon, for now.

---

I continue upwards, after healing on the bench for a while. I come across a wall of vines, which I slash through. And another. And another.

I look around. This place is a nest. A nest of those green bugs. Expect these ones haven’t hatched yet. I swing my needle at one experimentally. It bursts with a pop, releasing disgusting green slime.

And suddenly, a big one appears. Their mother. And she starts throwing rocks at me. And flying at me at Mach 1. I turn back, because I do not want to do this right now. But a sort of slime has blocked the ways in and out. And I realise there's only one way forward.

‘GARAMA!’ I scream as I launch into combat. The moss bug charges at me, but I dodge and I hit it with my needle to punish this weak, foolish, helpless bug-

My back breaks as a rock lands on me. That it threw at me.
And I can do nothing but contemplate how weak i've become as this stupid bug mauls me to death.
Well, I can do something.
I concentrate hard on forming a cocoon of silk around myself, until finally, everything goes dark in a blur of thread.

---
---

I open my eyes lying on the bench, with loose strands of silk still hanging off from my cloak.

'I am a loser.'

I run back up to the nest, and break apart the cocoon, absorbing the silk, even though I know I have nothing valuable I stored in there. I have to clean up the evidence of my previous defeat, don't I? A matter of honor.

The large moss bugs reappears, screaming at me as if to challenge me. I scream back equally as hard, and start slashing. It feels like slashing at Vespa again, my futile attempts at catching the hem of her royal robes, only for me to miss and get pinned down by my red cloak. Except this is not a royal bug. This isn't one of the three queens of Hallownest who raised me! This is a horrifying thing that deserves nothing but <ins>_death_<ins>!

I thrust my blunt needle into the hostile moss bug's large stomach, withdrawing it in a sweeping arc. The bug finally bursts apart in death, and I realise I forgot to give it a name. It was obviously the mother of those eggs, and it was the biggest moss creature out of them all. The Moss Mother. A fitting name.

---

I walk past the nest, before finding a ladder. But there also seems to be a strange door in front of me, with the patternings of the Snail Shamans on it. I try to open the door. It doesnt budge. I decided to climb the ladder.

Once I reach the top, I feel exhausted. I realise that I haven't had any proper rest in a few weeks. The few weeks after the Knight and The Hollow Knight fulfilled the purpose of the Shades and unified it under the Lord Of Shades. The kidnapping, the uncomfortable metal of the rune cage they put me in.
My head begins to spin, and i trudge along slower and slower, until I come to a stop. I see the shadow of a person, and i cry out for help. But all they do is giggle as I fall to the ground, unconcious.

---

Tap.
Tap, Tap.
'Up with you! Up! I won't have you dying on my floor!'
I wake up to a person beating me with a stick and bossing me around. Excellent. I get up and adopt my most agressive stance.

'Ahh, awake at last! You should mind your strength, traveller. There's no use wasting energy on an old nobody like me.',
says the maid. I can tell she is a maid by her simple, worn clothing. I take a deep breath and try to make my posh White Palace voice come to life. Time to prove I'm more Wyrm than Weaver. Ironic.

'I see that now, madam. My welcome to your land has not been kind. I had reason to be wary.' I think about that fight from before, and decide to not go into the details of my defeat.

'So you're another pilgrim then? Scuttled out of the darkness to climb the great path and bow at its gilded peak?' <ins>_HOW DARES THIS CRAZY WOMAN!!_<ins> To call me a common pilgrim, to assume I would like to grovel at the feet of a Higher Being! My father himself was a Higher being, beyond your understanding!
I try to be polite, anyways.

'I am no pilgrim. I am Hornet, and I arrive in your land by no choice of my own. I was brought to your kingdom by force, captured by strange bugs clad in veils.'
Now I've blown it. I've shown how weak I am to be kidnapped by 5 small bugs and a heavy cage to this maid.

'Oho! How curious! The bugs of the Citadel, at the summit of our land. They veil themselves in service to their faith, or perhaps as slaves to something greater...' Slaves? These bugs that managed to capture me, a demigod? SLAVES??? If these so-called slaves are so strong, then I must know who their master is.

'Then I shall seek their Citadel. If the veiled bugs would go to such lengths to bring me here, I intend to find out why.' And I intend to kill their leader. If a group of common slaves have been commanded to even come near me, then their leader is my sworn enemy.

'Oho! Ferocious! Intimidating! Yours is no pilgrim's spirit! You've the bearing of a hunter.' As if I wasn't a hunter before. As if I wasn't the sentinel of Hallownest, the grand slayer of the Vessels!

'Welcome then, fierce traveller, to Pharloom! Our holy yet haunted land.' Holy? This place, holy? I look around at the rocky, bony, gloomy atmosphere. The god who resides here must not be faring well. Doesn't stop me from wanting to kill them, of course. Weak prey is always preferable.

'Before you depart, permit an old bug a brief warning. Best keep that weapon drawn. Our kingdom is gripped by a curse. Its paths grow wild and dangerous, and though some still attempt the climb to the Citadel at its crest, none return to speak of what they see.' The Citadel. Interesting. I shall find my way up there and challenge the god that commands this kingdom, then. And if I find the nature of this curse, it may help me find out what this being is.
